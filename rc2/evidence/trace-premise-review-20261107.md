Any claim specifically about the executable also needs an explicit compilation/runtime trust statement.

Define the intended trace up to 223 by reading the 400 finite cells of `u` after initialization at index zero and after each successful call at indices 1 through 223. The generic Lean theorem accepts a total function on naturals; values beyond 223 can be arbitrary. Constructing this trace from a C execution, with all these reads and calls justified, is open.

## Binary64 nearest-even rounding under a common interpretation of the reals

Coq 8.20.1 with Flocq 4.2.1 checked the [Why3 1.8.2 realization theorem](why3-floor-even-c7JKUEAm/why3-1.8.2/lib/coq/Why3FloorEven.v). The [build and assumption report](why3-floor-even-c7JKUEAm/check.log) records successful coqchk and object SHA256 `d236d0970b896a520e160486203c7dfcd3d735693e83304a2e4ae8c1cdec74fa`. Both the nonzero theorem and the zero theorem report ClassicalDedekindReals.sig_not_dec, ClassicalDedekindReals.sig_forall_dec, FunctionalExtensionality.functional_extensionality_dep and Classical_Prop.classic.

Comparison with the [checked FloatLib theorem](c2-rounding-construction-20261107-121623/C2RoundingConstruction.lean) gives the same numerical rule. For nonzero x both statements take e satisfying 2^(e−1) ≤ |x| < 2^e, set k = max(e−53, −1074), and scale x by 2^(−k). Writing n for the floor of that signed quantity, both choose n below the half, n+1 above it, and the even one at equality, then multiply by 2^k. The fixed lower spacing covers subnormals. Each system separately proves zero maps to real zero. The external comparison identifies the two systems' mathematical reals, integer embeddings, order, powers of two, floor and parity. Neither kernel checks this identification.

For WP's projected double round, the [Cfloat model](c2-wp-postcondition-export-20261107/run-mUCtyS/model-cfloat.mlw) defines round_double(RNE,x) by to_f64(Float64.round RNE x). When the rounded result is in range, to_f64_range_round and Round_idempotent give its real projection as Float64.round RNE x. The common-formula result addresses that last real operation. Applying the projection argument throughout the expression still requires coverage of the relevant range conditions. It is a numerical-value claim and does not identify signed-zero bit patterns. The instrumented initial-grid evidence and the executable conditions above retain their scopes.

## Range audit of the WP casts in the benchmark reference update

This is a reviewed interval derivation for the source-pinned [composed expression](wp-reference-interpretation-08zrp8uz/probe.c), SHA256 `231047633ea7001e4c48fce8321bd72ca8f2bf17d9909247da36b56c140d70bc`. The ceiling arithmetic and parameter inequalities below were checked with exact rational arithmetic. The site-by-site argument is external to both kernels.

The stored parameters satisfy 0 < D = 5764607523034235/2^59 < 1/99, Q = 4150517416584649/2^64 ≥ 1/5000, and 0 < dt = 5170051590165233/2^60 ≤ 9/2000. For an absolute operand bound M in the table, both −M and M are exactly representable binary64 integers. Nearest-even rounding is monotone and fixes those endpoints, so the same bound holds after rounding, including for subnormal inputs and ties. This gives an induction from the loaded cell values through the actual operation order. For WP's extra conversion in round_double, Bounded_real_no_overflow puts the rounded value in range; to_f64_range_round, to_f64_range_finite and Round_idempotent then give the finite decoded result. The [WP float model](c2-wp-postcondition-export-20261107/run-mUCtyS/model-cfloat.mlw) and [Why3 IEEE model](c2-wp-postcondition-export-20261107/run-mUCtyS/model-ieee_float.mlw) supply these rules.

| Operand before each Euler rounding, using the preceding rounded value | First-stage magnitude bound | Second-stage magnitude bound |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2 times center | 4 | 8 |
| Right minus rounded double-center | 6 | 12 |
| Add left | 8 | 16 |
| Divide by positive stored Q | 40,000 | 80,000 |

The initial row has magnitude ≤ 2. All three first-stage neighbor evaluations use that row, including repeated indices at a clamped boundary. Their outputs have magnitude ≤ 4, which supplies the three second-stage input bounds. The estimates in the D row round 40,000/99 and 80,000/99 outward to integers. The timestep row similarly bounds 405 × 9/2000 and 809 × 9/2000. For the final average, even the previously WP-proved looser second-stage output bound 12 suffices: the two half-products have magnitude ≤ 1 and ≤ 6, and the sum of their rounded results has magnitude ≤ 7. These three endpoints are also exactly representable. The checked [stage and average contracts](wp-reference-interpretation-tfsopvv2/result.json) use 12; the tighter second-stage 8 here is an interval deduction. Reapplication on successive rows uses the C4 reference range and the WP loop invariant; the bound 7 on the final addition alone would not establish the next row's range.

Parameter and entry conversions are separate from the ten operation positions. The diffusivity literal 1/100 lies in [0,1]; its stored dyadic agrees with A6. The ratio 3/200 and its rounded square lie in [0,1], as does 1/223. The printed WP goal gives exactly the stored Q and dt above. Literals 0.5 and 2 are exact in binary64. Entry conversion needs the [-2,2] range and a rounding fixed point, as in [reference_decode](wp-reference-interpretation-_vh9ra7u/wp.log). Finite C4 cells supply that fixed point on the FloatLib side. Every reference-update cast thus has numerical range coverage under the previously recorded common interpretation. This audit adds no claim about malloc, libm initialization, compiler behavior, or bitwise signed zero.
